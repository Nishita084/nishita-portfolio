Flagship case study — Distributor Onboarding Workflow Redesign

Enterprise Operations · Distributor Onboarding · Process Improvement

Confidentiality note: Project details have been anonymized and adapted to protect client and organizational confidentiality.

Context

A large enterprise distributor network was managing new-distributor onboarding through a manual, multi-handoff process involving email, spreadsheets, and disconnected approval steps. As the network scaled, onboarding time and errors increased, creating operational friction and limited visibility across the process.

Problem

Business stakeholders could describe the pain — onboarding took too long, data was inconsistent across systems, and approvals stalled without visibility — but the underlying challenges had not been translated into a clear set of requirements that a delivery team could act on. My role was to close that gap.

01
Elicitation
Stakeholder interviews and workshops across operations, IT, and distributor-facing teams to understand the actual process, pain points, dependencies, and business expectations.
02
AS-IS mapping
Mapped the existing onboarding workflow across teams and systems, identifying manual handoffs, approval bottlenecks, data dependencies, and ownership gaps.
03
Requirements
Translated stakeholder needs and process findings into structured business and functional requirements, documenting the redesigned workflow through BRD/FRD artifacts.
04
TO-BE design
Designed a streamlined future-state workflow with consolidated approval steps, clearer ownership, improved visibility, and reduced dependency on manual coordination.
05
UAT & sign-off
Supported UAT with business stakeholders by defining test scenarios, tracking defects, validating fixes, and supporting business sign-off before go-live.

My role

  • Owned requirement gathering end-to-end across business and IT stakeholders
  • Authored BRD/FRD documentation for the redesigned workflow
  • Led UAT planning, execution, and stakeholder sign-off
  • Tracked delivery through JIRA / Azure DevOps against Agile sprints

Risks & assumptions

  • Assumed distributor-facing teams could absorb a changed approval flow without added headcount
  • Flagged data-consistency risks between the onboarding tool and downstream systems early, before UAT
  • Validated assumptions in workshops rather than carrying them silently into the FRD

Secondary case — Shipment Visibility & Report Personalisation

Teams working with shipment data had different reporting needs across operations, account management, and distributor support. A single generic report made it difficult for each team to quickly access the information relevant to their workflows. The challenge was to understand these differences and translate them into a more usable, role-specific reporting experience.

Confidentiality note · Project details have been anonymized and adapted to protect client and organizational confidentiality.

Before
  • One generic report layout used across teams
  • Teams relied on manual status checks and follow-ups
  • Limited visibility into shipment exceptions
  • Different teams had different reporting requirements
After
  • Reporting layout aligned to individual team requirements
  • Shipment information surfaced based on user needs
  • Exceptions made easier to identify and follow up
  • One shared data foundation supporting multiple views
Problem

Stakeholders lacked an efficient way to access shipment information relevant to their specific workflows. The existing reporting experience did not account for differences in how each team consumed and acted on shipment data.

Approach

Gathered requirements from different consuming teams, identified their common and team-specific information needs, and translated them into functional requirements for a personalised reporting layout. Worked with stakeholders to validate the proposed experience and ensure the requirements were clear for delivery.

Outcome

Created a more user-focused reporting experience by aligning shipment visibility with how different teams actually consumed the data, while maintaining a shared underlying dataset rather than creating separate reports for each team.
